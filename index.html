Discuss your first move with a partner. Then choose an action to see what it could mean.

Reveal the warning signs
  • Pressure: a 30-minute deadline tries to rush your decision.
  • A reward: the gift distracts you from the account warning.
  • A secret request: someone wants your password and sign-in code.
  • An unverified destination: a familiar-looking link or sender name does not prove a message is genuine.

A polished message can still be a scam. Verify through the official app, a saved bookmark, or a teacher-approved address you already know.

Build three everyday habits.

Small choices. Stronger accounts.

01 / Protect

Make each account unique.

Use a long, unique password with a trusted password manager, or a passkey where available. Follow school rules for school accounts.

02 / Verify

Add another layer.

Turn on multifactor authentication (MFA). Never share a sign-in code or approve a login you did not start.

03 / Recover

Have a way back in.

Keep recovery options current. If something goes wrong, tell a trusted adult or school IT promptly. Getting help is the right move.

Ready to secure the whole account?

Learn the habits, plan your response, and try the knowledge check.
